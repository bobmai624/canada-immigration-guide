  <h2>Overview</h2><p>The Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream is an economic immigration program that nominates talented and qualified foreign educated graduates from outside Canada for permanent residence upon launching start-up enterprises and innovative businesses in Alberta. The Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream is a collaboration between the Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) and approved designated agencies.</p><p>Foreign graduates interested in applying under the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream must work with a designated agency. The designated agency will issue a letter of recommendation if it deems that the foreign graduate meets <abbr title="Alberta Advantage Immigration Program">AAIP</abbr> requirements. The foreign graduate must submit the letter of recommendation with their Expression of Interest (EOI) through the AAIP Portal.</p><p>The designated agency will review business plans based on the foreign graduate’s ability to demonstrate market need or demand, the potential for successful market entry in the short-term to medium-term, customer acquisition, business development, key partnerships and financial plans to fund the development and operation of the start-up. The designated agency will provide a written report on their assessment of the proposed business plan. Foreign graduates must submit this report with their Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream Business Application.</p><h3>Approved designated agencies</h3><p>The role of the designated agency is to:</p><ul><li>support the marketing of Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream</li><li>determine the entrepreneurial readiness of a foreign graduate and the potential success of the start-up by reviewing the business idea and provide support on the development of a business plan</li><li>select foreign graduates to recommend for AAIP</li><li>connect foreign graduates with the appropriate networks, and provide business counselling, coaching and guidance when launching their business in Alberta and while they are trying to achieve the requirements of the Business Performance An AAIP officer may contact you for more information.</p><p>AAIP does not guarantee that all complete Business Applications received will be assessed or that all candidates who meet the assessment criteria will be issued a nomination. Review the declaration for the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Start-up Visa Stream available on the AAIP Portal and <a href="/aaip-foreign-graduate-entrepreneur-stream-how-to-apply">AAIP Terms and Conditions</a> for complete information.</p><p>Submission of a Business Application or nomination by AAIP does not guarantee you will be issued a permanent resident visa.</p><h2>Ineligibility</h2><p>You are not eligible to apply under Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream if:</p><ul><li>you have an active nomination, which has not expired and/or is eligible for extension under another <abbr title="Alberta Advantage Immigration Program">AAIP</abbr> stream – candidates are only eligible for one AAIP Nomination Certificate</li><li>you have been issued a letter on a previous AAIP application that stated you are not eligible to re-apply to AAIP for a specified period of time – if you submit an application during the specified period, it will be declined</li><li>you have a removal order issued against you by <abbr title="Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada">IRCC</abbr> or Canada Border Services Agency</li><li>you are a refugee claimant or an individual involved in IRCC appeal or removal process – AAIP does not intervene in IRCC refugee claim, appeal or removal process</li><li>you are a foreign national living or working in Canada with no valid temporary resident status</li><li>your business is on the list of ineligible businesses listed below</li></ul><h2>Established businesses in Alberta</h2><p>Foreign graduates who currently own and operate a business in Alberta can apply to <abbr title="Alberta Advantage Immigration Program">AAIP</abbr> as a pathway for permanent residency. They must demonstrate they meet the criteria highlighted in the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream Points Grid and Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream mandatory minimums grid at the time of <abbr title="Expression of Interest">EOI</abbr> submission. The established business must have operated in Alberta for at least one year immediately prior to submitting an EOI in the AAIP portal.</p><p>EOI submissions will be scored based on the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream Points Grid and Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream mandatory minimums grid and candidates with the highest-ranking points will be requested to submit a Business Application to AAIP. Foreign graduates who have already owned and operated their business in Alberta will not be required to operate their business for an additional one year after applying to AAIP.</p><p>Eligible foreign graduates will be issued a nomination certificate after submitting their Business Application to AAIP and must continue to operate their business in Alberta while they go through the permanent residence application process with IRCC.</p><p>Read <a href="/aaip-foreign-graduate-entrepreneur-stream-after-you-are-nominated">After you are nominated</a> for more information on next steps.</p><h2>Business eligibility</h2><h3>Economic establishment</h3><p>Candidates must have a strong likelihood of becoming economically established in Alberta. AAIP may decline an application if it determines that a candidate is unlikely to economically establish in Alberta.</p><p>For the Entrepreneur Streams, economic establishment means demonstrating a credible and substantial likelihood that you will establish, actively manage, operate and sustain a viable business in Alberta as an owner-operator, in accordance with the requirements of your stream.</p><p>AAIP assesses economic establishment using relevant, credible and verifiable information available at the time of assessment.</p><p>When assessing economic establishment, AAIP may consider factors including:</p><ul><li>Alberta business presence: How you will actively manage the business on a day-to-day basis in Alberta as an owner-operator, including operating from business premises where required.</li><li>Business viability and sustainability: Whether the proposed or acquired business has a reasonable prospect of operating successfully, remaining financially sustainable and adapting to market conditions in Alberta.</li><li>Economic contribution to Alberta: The expected economic benefit of the business to Alberta, which may include investment, job creation, business expansion, innovation, export development, regional diversification or other economic contributions.</li><li>Employment and workforce planning: Whether proposed workforce plans, including hiring and salaries, are reasonable in relation to the scale, nature, operational needs, financial projections and growth plans of the business. AAIP may also consider relevant Alberta labour market information.</li><li>Applicant capacity and business alignment: Your relevant experience, knowledge, financial capacity and managerial competency to establish, operate and grow the proposed business.</li><li>Regulatory readiness: Whether you have considered applicable federal, provincial and municipal laws, regulations, licensing requirements and industry standards relevant to the proposed business and how those requirements will be met.</li></ul><p>Information provided at the time of submitting your Entrepreneur Expression of Interest, Business Application, throughout the assessment process, and during any subsequent monitoring and reporting period, as applicable, may be used to assess economic establishment. AAIP may request additional information where needed to complete the assessment.</p><h3>Eligible businesses</h3><p>It is your responsibility to ensure your proposed business meets the criteria for eligible businesses. If it is not an eligible business or does not clearly demonstrate a significant economic benefit to Alberta, your Business Application may be declined.</p><p>The proposed business must have the potential for creating economic benefit to Alberta, in the opinion of and as determined by AAIP. A proposed business must align with the following requirements to be eligible under the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream:</p><ul><li>The purpose of the proposed business must be to make a profit from active or earned income, not passive income, through the sale of goods or provision of services.</li><li>The proposed business must comply with any applicable federal, provincial or municipal legal, regulatory or licensing requirements that govern its operation. This includes Alberta labour laws, including, but not limited to, employment standards, occupational health and safety, and labour relations legislations.</li><li>The proposed business must comply with applicable trade certification requirements in Alberta. For example, businesses in a compulsory trade must have a valid Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Trade (AIT) recognized trade certificate. To determine if you require a trade certificate, review these lists of designated trades:<ul><li><a href="https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/compulsory-certification-trades" target="_blank">Compulsory Certification Trades</a></li><li><a href="https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/optional-certification-trades/" target="_blank">Optional Certification Trades and Occupations</a></li></ul></li><li>The business plan or pitch deck must indicate the proposed business is connected to one of the following sectors: technology, aerospace, financial services, energy, agriculture, tourism, life sciences, and pharmaceuticals.</li><li>The proposed business must have a physical place of business in Alberta at all times.</li><li>You must own a minimum of 34% of the business in an urban centre, or a minimum of 51% of the business in a regional area. You are required to reside in Alberta and be involved in the day-to-day management of the business. You may not do this remotely, or from another Canadian province or territory or from another country, and you are required to demonstrate residence in Alberta while on a work permit.</li></ul><h3>Ineligible businesses</h3><p>Ineligible businesses include:</p><ul><li>any business in contravention of the <em>Immigration and Refugee Protection Act</em> and Regulations – for example, immigration-linked investment schemes or passive investment</li><li>businesses without a value-add economic component, including:<ul><li>pay day loan, cheque cashing and related businesses</li><li>businesses trading in used goods</li></ul></li><li>businesses that would be considered passive investment or lack active management, including: insurance brokerage or business brokerage</li><li>project based or seasonal businesses</li><li>home-based businesses, including:<ul><li>businesses that are not zoned for commercial or industrial operations</li><li>bed-and-breakfasts and lodging houses</li></ul></li><li>businesses that are part of a succession plan</li><li>businesses involved in producing, distributing or selling pornography or sexually explicit products or services, or providing sexually oriented services</li><li>any other type of business that by association would tend to bring AAIP and the Government of Alberta into disrepute, in the opinion of and as determined by AAIP</li></ul>

  </div>
