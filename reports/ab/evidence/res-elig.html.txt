  <h2>Check your eligibility for AAIP</h2><p>Before you begin, make sure you are eligible to apply for AAIP and what stream you should apply for.</p><p><a class="goa-cta" href="https://aaip.alberta.ca/eligibility-explorer" target="_blank">Use the AAIP Eligibility Explorer</a></p>

  </div>


  
  <div class="paragraph paragraph--type--text-block paragraph--view-mode--default goa-column-100-100-100">
    
  <h2>Overview</h2><p>The Rural Entrepreneur Stream allows Alberta to nominate qualified and capable entrepreneurs who want to start a new or buy an existing business in a rural Alberta community.</p><h3>Entrepreneurs</h3><p>An entrepreneur is a person who sets up a business with the aim to make a profit. An entrepreneur is commonly seen as an innovator, a source of new ideas, goods, services, and business/or procedures.</p><p>Entrepreneurs play a key role in Alberta’s economy, using their skills and initiative to anticipate needs and create jobs for Albertans. Entrepreneurs who prove to be successful in taking on the risks of establishing a business are rewarded with profits and continued growth opportunities.</p><p>The Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) defines rural Alberta communities as any community with a population of less than 100,000. The AAIP uses the most recent census from <a href="https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2016/as-sa/fogs-spg/Facts-pr-eng.cfm?Lang=Eng&amp;GK=PR&amp;GC=48&amp;TOPIC=1" target="_blank">Statistics Canada</a> to determine a community’s population. In addition, the community must be outside of the Calgary and Edmonton Census Metropolitan Area. More information can be found at:</p><ul><li><a href="https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2016/as-sa/fogs-spg/Facts-cma-eng.cfm?LANG=eng&amp;GK=CMA&amp;GC=825" target="_blank">Calgary Census Metropolitan Area</a></li><li><a href="https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2016/as-sa/fogs-spg/Facts-cma-eng.cfm?LANG=Eng&amp;GK=CMA&amp;GC=835" target="_blank">Edmonton Census Metropolitan Area</a></li><li>rural Alberta communities information is in the <a href="/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/lbr-aaip-rural-entrepreneur-stream-community-guide.pdf" target="_blank" data-entity-type="media" data-entity-uuid="62b7523d-ca55-437c-8cb3-622551cff160" data-entity-substitution="media" title="lbr-aaip-rural-entrepreneur-stream-community-guide.pdf">Rural Entrepreneur Stream Community Guide</a></li></ul><h3>Participating communities</h3><p>Candidates interested in a specific rural Alberta community should connect with the community contact person to discuss their business proposal and schedule an exploratory visit. Each community&nbsp;has their own process for scheduling an exploratory visit. Communities reserve the right to meet only with potential entrepreneurs whom they deem as a fit.</p><p>When contacting a community, use only the contact details listed in each community’s profile. Communities not listed but wanting to participate can submit the <a href="https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/form/AINP12664" target="_blank">Community Enrolment Form</a> to <a href="/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection#7d1813090f180d0f181318080f530e080d0d120f090e180f0b141e183d1a120b531c1f531e1c"><span class="__cf_email__" data-cfemail="5e3b302a2c3b2e2c3b303b2b2c702d2b2e2e312c2a2d3b2c28373d3b1e393128703f3c703d3f">[email&#160;protected]</span></a>.</p><p>For more information on participating rural communities, see the <a href="/aaip-rural-entrepreneur-stream-participating-communities">participating communities</a> page.</p>

  </div>

  
  <div class="paragraph paragraph--type--text-block paragraph--view-mode--default goa-column-100-100-100">
    
  <h2>Exploratory visit</h2><p>Candidates must complete an exploratory visit. The community and the candidate determine together if the exploratory visit will be in-person or completed using web-based video conferencing. Candidates are required to submit an Exploratory Visit Report with the Business Application whether the visit was conducted in-person or by web-based video conferencing.</p><p>Candidates completing an in-person exploratory visit need to meet <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada.html?outside" target="_blank">visa requirements</a> to be able to come to Alberta. The <abbr title="Alberta Advantage Immigration Program">AAIP</abbr> and the community will not provide you with a letter of support to obtain a visa for the exploratory visit.</p><p>Prior to coming to Alberta for the exploratory visit, we recommend candidates research current market opportunities in the community, licensing and registration requirements, and steps involved in establishing a business in Alberta. The exploratory visit provides you with the opportunity to meet with local business contacts, economic development officers from the community as well as any other prospective contacts to learn and understand the local business environment, and requirements to build a successful business in the community. The purpose of the exploratory visit is to assist you in making an informed decision about operating and managing a business in a rural Alberta community. Keep records of the trip, including any contacts and meetings, as this information is needed for the Exploratory Visit Report.</p><p>The Exploratory Visit Report must include the following information:</p><ul><li>The name of the professional business service providers or settlement organizations you visited during your trip or spoke to in your video conference, including mailing addresses, telephone numbers and emails.</li><li>A description of the activity, in-person meeting or video conference and how it relates to your business establishment or settlement.</li><li>Copies of business cards you collected from relevant contact and business service providers contacted during your in-person visit (if applicable).</li><li>Details of visits to existing business locations and meetings with current owners if you are planning to purchase an existing business. You should include the business name, location and the current owners’ names and contact details.</li></ul><p>After the exploratory visit, provide a copy of the complete <a href="https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/form/AINP12666" target="_blank">Rural Entrepreneur Stream Business Proposal Summary</a> to the community contact person. This allows the community to reaffirm the discussions during the exploratory visit. The Business Proposal Summary provides information on the business venture in the community. If the community deems that you and your business proposal is a benefit to the community, a Community Support Letter is issued, and the community endorses the Business Proposal Summary. The Community Support Letter and endorsement of the Business Proposal Summary ensures the community is aware and supportive of the business, and supports your retention in Alberta. The community may provide you with additional information on contacts and support available for you to transition into the community upon arrival.</p><p>After receiving a copy of the Community Support Letter and endorsed Business Proposal Summary from the community, you can submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) to the AAIP.</p>

criteria and your EOI Points are verified. A Program Officer from the AAIP may contact you for more information.</p><p>The AAIP does not guarantee that all complete Business Applications received will be assessed or that all candidates who meet the assessment criteria will be issued a Business Application Approval Letter and/or nomination certificate. Review the declaration&nbsp;for the Rural Entrepreneur Stream available on the AAIP Portal and the&nbsp;<a href="/aaip-rural-entrepreneur-stream-how-to-apply">Program’s Terms and Conditions</a>&nbsp;for complete information. Submission of a Business Application or nomination by the AAIP does not guarantee you will be issued a permanent resident visa.</p><h2>Ineligibility</h2><p>You are not eligible to apply under the Rural Entrepreneur Stream if:</p><ul><li>you have an active nomination, which has not expired and/or is eligible for extension under another AAIP stream. Candidates are only eligible for one AAIP Nomination Certificate</li><li>you have been issued a letter on a previous AAIP application that stated you are not eligible to re-apply to the Program for a specified period of time. You will be declined under the Rural Entrepreneur Stream if you apply during the specified period.</li><li>you have a removal order issued against you <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship.html" target="_blank">by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC)</a> or Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)</li><li>you are a refugee claimant or an individual involved in IRCC appeal or removal process. AAIP does not intervene in IRCC refugee claim, appeal or removal process.</li><li>you are a foreign national living or working in Canada with no valid temporary resident status</li><li>your business is on the list of ineligible businesses</li></ul>

  <h2><a class="ck-anchor" id="established-businesses-eligibility-criteria"></a>Established businesses in Alberta</h2><p>Recognizing the economic contributions by foreign entrepreneurs in Alberta who currently own and operate a business in Alberta, the <abbr title="Alberta Advantage Immigration Program">AAIP</abbr> is providing an immigration pathway for these entrepreneurs to apply for permanent residency.</p><p>Entrepreneurs who currently own and operate a business in a rural Alberta community can apply to the AAIP and must demonstrate they meet the criteria highlighted in the Rural Entrepreneur Stream Points grid at the time of EOI submission. The established business must have operated in Alberta for at least one year immediately prior to submitting an EOI in the AAIP portal. Furthermore, the Community Support letter must be included with the <abbr title="Expression of Interest">EOI</abbr> submission.</p><p>EOI submissions are scored based on the Rural Entrepreneur Stream Points grid and candidates with the highest-ranking points are requested to submit a Business Application to the AAIP. Entrepreneurs who have already established businesses in rural Alberta communities and are currently operating them are not required to operate their business for an additional one year after applying to the AAIP. Eligible entrepreneurs are issued a nomination certificate after submitting their Business Application to the Program and must continue to operate their business in Alberta while they go through the permanent residence application process with <abbr title="Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada">IRCC</abbr>. Refer to <a href="/aaip-rural-entrepreneur-stream-after-you-are-nominated">after you are nominated</a> for more information on next steps.</p><h2>Business eligibility</h2><h3>Economic establishment</h3><p>Candidates must have a strong likelihood of becoming economically established in Alberta. AAIP may decline an application if it determines that a candidate is unlikely to economically establish in Alberta.</p><p>For the Entrepreneur Streams, economic establishment means demonstrating a credible and substantial likelihood that you will establish, actively manage, operate and sustain a viable business in Alberta as an owner-operator, in accordance with the requirements of your stream.</p><p>AAIP assesses economic establishment using relevant, credible and verifiable information available at the time of assessment.</p><p>When assessing economic establishment, AAIP may consider factors including:</p><ul><li>Alberta business presence: How you will actively manage the business on a day-to-day basis in Alberta as an owner-operator, including operating from business premises where required.</li><li>Business viability and sustainability: Whether the proposed or acquired business has a reasonable prospect of operating successfully, remaining financially sustainable and adapting to market conditions in Alberta.</li><li>Economic contribution to Alberta: The expected economic benefit of the business to Alberta, which may include investment, job creation, business expansion, innovation, export development, regional diversification or other economic contributions.</li><li>Employment and workforce planning: Whether proposed workforce plans, including hiring and salaries, are reasonable in relation to the scale, nature, operational needs, financial projections and growth plans of the business. AAIP may also consider relevant Alberta labour market information.</li><li>Applicant capacity and business alignment: Your relevant experience, knowledge, financial capacity and managerial competency to establish, operate and grow the proposed business.</li><li>Regulatory readiness: Whether you have considered applicable federal, provincial and municipal laws, regulations, licensing requirements and industry standards relevant to the proposed business and how those requirements will be met.</li></ul><p>Information provided at the time of submitting your Entrepreneur Expression of Interest, Business Application, throughout the assessment process, and during any subsequent monitoring and reporting period, as applicable, may be used to assess economic establishment. AAIP may request additional information where needed to complete the assessment.</p><h3>Eligible businesses</h3><p>It is your responsibility to ensure your proposed business meets the criteria for eligible businesses. If it is not an eligible business or does not clearly demonstrate a significant economic benefit to Alberta, your EOI or Business Application may be declined.</p><p>Business must have the potential for creating economic benefit to Alberta, as in the opinion of and as determined by the AAIP. A proposed business must align with the following general requirements to be eligible under the Rural Entrepreneur Stream:</p><ul><li>The purpose of the proposed business must be to make a profit from active or earned income, not passive income, through the sale of goods and/or provision of services.</li><li>The proposed business must comply with any applicable Government of Canada, Government of Alberta or municipal legal, regulatory, or licensing requirements that govern its operation. This includes Alberta labour laws, including but not limited to, employment standards, occupational health and safety and labour relations legislations.</li><li>The proposed business must comply with applicable trade certification requirements in Alberta. For example, businesses in a certification required trade must have a valid Alberta Apprenticeship and Industry Trade (AIT) recognized trade certificate. To determine if you require a trade certificate, review these lists of designated trades:<ul><li><a href="https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/compulsory-certification-trades" target="_blank">Certification Required Trades</a></li><li><a href="https://tradesecrets.alberta.ca/trades-in-alberta/optional-certification-trades/" target="_blank">Certification Optional Trades</a></li></ul></li><li>The proposed business must have a physical place of business in a rural Alberta community at all times.</li><li>For franchises, the ownership requirements and all other program criteria are the same as for other businesses. Candidates must also provide evidence that they have received support from the master franchisor to operate a franchise location.</li><li>You must own at least 51% of the business if it is a new business, or have 100% ownership of the business if you are purchasing an already established business (business succession). You are required to reside in Alberta and be involved in the day-to-day management of the business. You may not do this remotely, from another Canadian province or territory, or from another country and you are required to demonstrate residence in Alberta while on a work permit.</li></ul><h3>Ineligible businesses</h3><p>Ineligible businesses include:</p><ul><li>any business in contravention of the&nbsp;<em>Immigration and Refugee Protection Act&nbsp;</em>and Regulations, for example, immigration-linked investment schemes or passive investment</li><li>businesses without a value-add economic component, including:<ul><li>pay day loan, cheque cashing and related businesses</li><li>businesses trading in used goods</li></ul></li><li>businesses that would be considered passive investment or lack active management, including:<ul><li>property rental, investment, and leasing activities</li><li>real estate development/brokerage, insurance brokerage or business brokerage</li><li>coin-operated businesses, including laundromats and car wash operations</li></ul></li><li>project based or seasonal businesses</li><li>home-based businesses, including:<ul><li>businesses that are not zoned for commercial or industrial operations</li><li>bed-and-breakfasts and lodging houses</li></ul></li><li>businesses that are part of a succession plan and:<ul><li>are owned or operated by a relative of the candidate</li><li>were previously owned/operated by a current or former AAIP entrepreneur stream candidate/nominee within the past 4 years</li><li>have changed ownership within the past 3 years prior to the Rural Entrepreneur Stream Business Application</li></ul></li><li>businesses involved in producing, distributing, or selling pornography or sexually explicit products or services, or providing sexually oriented services</li><li>any other type of business that by association would tend to bring the AAIP and the Government of Alberta into disrepute, in the opinion of and as determined by the AAIP</li></ul>

  </div>


  </div>
